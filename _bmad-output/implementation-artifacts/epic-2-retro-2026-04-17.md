## What Went Well
1. End-to-end pipeline was delivered incrementally with clear contracts between stories (RAG → LLM → orchestration → doctor service → API).
2. Fallback design worked as intended: triage path aims to always return a valid response shape.
3. Quality improved through strong review loops; multiple high-impact issues were detected and fixed before closure.
4. Interface boundaries were generally respected (router thinness, service-level business logic, schema-based contracts).
5. Story records captured concrete implementation details, making cross-story analysis and handoff easier.

## Challenges and Friction
1. Repeated review findings show late discovery of defects in critical paths (validation, fallback behavior, contract fidelity).
2. Several reliability concerns were explicitly deferred across stories (queue growth, startup observability, performance optimization).
3. Story 2.5 required correction of pre-existing architectural violations in the triage router before final compliance.
4. Runtime/environment coupling remained a source of friction (dependency availability and local environment quirks).

## Cross-Story Patterns Observed
- **Review-led stabilization:** substantial quality gains came after review rounds, not first-pass implementations.
- **Deferred-risk accumulation:** multiple “defer” decisions clustered around resilience/performance concerns.
- **Contract hardening trend:** output schema and fallback semantics became progressively stricter from Story 2.2 onward.
- **Safety posture consistency:** recurring emphasis on no symptom-text leakage and controlled error handling.

## Technical Debt and Deferred Work

2. RAG startup observability gap: service may appear healthy while corpus seeding failed (deferred in Story 2.1).
3. RAG service performance debt: repeated client/model setup on retrieval path (deferred in Story 2.1).
4. LLM service performance debt: chain reconstruction per request (deferred in Story 2.2).
